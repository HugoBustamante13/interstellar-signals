The challenge

"Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions". Interstellar Signals is an aid for each astronaut to assess their own health: you log your measurements, see your status as a traffic light on a human figure, review your trends and get action guidance when something falls outside what is expected.

The tool does not replace the crew medical officer: it helps each astronaut know when a change is normal for them, when to repeat a measurement and when to report it.

Distance is the underlying argument: with communication delays of up to 20 minutes to Earth, the crew has to be able to decide the first steps on its own.

NASA HRP hazards and indicators

NASA's Human Research Program groups the risks of a long trip into five hazards. This is how they show up in the app:

HazardWhat it meansIndicators
Altered gravityThe body adapts: heart, muscle, bone, inner ear.Heart and circulation; bone and muscle: resting HR, blood pressure, exercise, fitness

From measurement to decision

The idea is to rely on equipment already used in flight. The shirt watches daily, the watch covers what the shirt does not do well, and ultrasound only comes in when something is red. In this prototype all of that data is simulated or manual.

SourceWhat it providesReal precedent
Smart shirtHR, breathing, SpO₂, skin temperature and activity, continuously.Bio-Monitor (Astroskin) from the Canadian agency, used on the ISS in sessions of about 72 hours.
RingOxygen saturation at the finger.To be verified: we have not confirmed which ring has been used in flight.
WristwatchSleep by actigraphy and light exposure, over weeks.Actiwatch on the ISS: 21 astronauts and 3,248 days of recording.
ManualCuff blood pressure, glucose, mood, exercise and the list of immune signs.The shirt only estimates pressure; the direct measurement is used to alert.
UltrasoundDiagnosis when something is red, not daily watching.On the ISS a crew member does it, guided remotely by an expert on Earth.
  • Green: you see your panel, keep to your routine and mention it in your weekly private medical conference.
  • Yellow: you repeat the measurement and follow the guidance. If it does not return to normal, you tell the onboard crew medical officer.
  • Red, now: you follow the guidance and tell the crew medical officer, who decides the first steps and can request an ultrasound.
  • Red, once there is a link: the flight surgeon on Earth confirms or corrects. Near Earth that is almost immediate; toward Mars it can take a while, which is why the first steps are taken onboard.

Two of the challenge's four problems have no direct daily measurement. Immune defenses are watched through signs (temperature and a weekly list) and bone through what protects it (strength exercise). The app says so on each card.

Traffic light rules

A fixed Earth threshold gives false alarms in flight, because the body changes: blood pressure drops about 8–9 mmHg (Norsk 2015). That is why each astronaut is compared with their own baseline.

  • Personal band: mean ± 2.5 standard deviations of the last 28 days, with at least 14 readings.
  • Yellow: 3 readings in a row outside the band. Also HR below 50 bpm, SpO₂ 3 points below the baseline, or fewer than 3 exercise sessions in 7 days.
  • Red: an absolute clinical limit, a 4-deviation jump confirmed the next day, or two indicators in yellow at the same time.
  • First 14 flight days: the body is adapting, so only the clinical limits count.

Across 200 simulated missions the traffic light gave 1.82 false episodes per mission and flagged 62% of the anomalies that the simulator itself invented. It is a measure against synthetic data, not against astronauts.

What we do not claim

  • All data are synthetic. The simulator is not a validated physiological model. Zamar and Carlos are demo names for two simulated astronauts, not real people.
  • NASA does not publish numeric alarm criteria. The red limits are clinical references from Earth; the bands, the days in a row and the jumps are team assumptions.
  • The action guidance is guidance only and does not replace the crew medical officer.

Architecture

  • Sensors: in the demo, an ESP32 plays the role of the shirt (heart rate, SpO₂ and temperature). On a mission they would be a Bio-Monitor-type shirt and an actigraphy watch.
  • This page: HTML and Three.js, no framework; it is what you see in the demo. The colors of each mission day are computed by semaforo.py in the simulator and embedded when the page is generated. Live readings and the check-in are evaluated here, in the browser, with the same clinical limits and the same personal band.
  • Live demo: a phone acts as shirt, ring, watch and manual inputs, and sends simulated readings.
  • Backend: FastAPI and SQLite. It serves this page, bridges to the phone in the live demo and stores the records of the React app.
  • React app: TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind and Recharts. It logs measurements against the backend and shows their history.
  • Simulator: Python. It generates the synthetic missions and evaluates the traffic light.

Open items

  • Cumulative radiation, SANS, bone density and vestibular function remain uncovered.
  • The immune signs rule (two signs, or one with 38 °C) is a team assumption and has no simulated history.
  • The shirt measures skin temperature, not core: the fever limits need confirmation with a thermometer.
  • Confirm where the temperature sensor measures: it changes the 38.0 and 38.5 °C limits.
  • Decide whether blood pressure counts as a single indicator in the two-yellows rule.
